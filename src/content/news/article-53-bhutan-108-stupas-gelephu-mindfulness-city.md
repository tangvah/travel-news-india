*Based on a Press Information Bureau release dated 1 November 2026 and a post by the Ministry of External Affairs spokesperson on 3 November. Checked on 4 November 2026 (IST).*

Bhutan has built and consecrated **108 Buddhist stupas** (Jangchub Chortens) in a single day at **Gelephu Mindfulness City**, the new development in southern Bhutan near the border with Assam. According to [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2331224&lang=1&reg=1), thousands of volunteers put up the stupas on 1 November 2026. Each is 15 metres tall, and they stand 108 metres apart along an **11.7 km stretch of the Aie (Mau) River**. The [MEA spokesperson](https://x.com/MEAIndia/status/2117651587275637041) said the work was done in **six hours**.

The project was conceived and led by the King of Bhutan, Jigme Khesar Namgyel Wangchuck. PIB says it aims to present Gelephu Mindfulness City as a major centre of Vajrayana Buddhism and of Bhutanese culture.

## India's role

Union Minister Kiren Rijiju represented the Government of India at the ceremony, which the King led. The King, Bhutan's Prime Minister and Mr Rijiju prayed at **Stupa No. 76**, which the King dedicated to Prime Minister Narendra Modi for his support of the Gelephu project.

During his visit from 31 October to 2 November, Mr Rijiju met the King and Prime Minister Tshering Tobgay. PIB says the two sides discussed cooperation in development, trade, energy, investment, connectivity and culture. Mr Rijiju restated India's support for Gelephu Mindfulness City and its role in developing neighbouring areas of India together with Bhutan.

## Why it matters for travellers from India

Gelephu sits close to Assam, so the site will interest travellers from Northeast India. However, the official releases do not say whether or when visitors can see the stupas. This is a new landmark, not a change to travel rules. Before planning a trip, check Bhutan's current entry and fee rules on official Bhutanese government sites, since this announcement says nothing about them.
