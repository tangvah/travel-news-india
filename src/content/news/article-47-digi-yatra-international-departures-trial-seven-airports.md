*Based on a Ministry of Civil Aviation release issued through PIB on 1 November 2026 and IndiGo's press release of the same date. Checked on 3 November 2026 (IST).*

Digi Yatra, the face-recognition boarding system that Indian flyers already use on domestic flights, is now being **trialled on international departures**. The [Ministry of Civil Aviation said on 1 November](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2331200&lang=1&reg=3) that trials have started at **seven airports** and will run from **November to December 2026**.

## Key points

- **Airports:** Delhi, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Cochin (Kochi), Mumbai, Chennai and Amritsar.
- **Who can use it:** eligible **Indian nationals holding e-passports** who are flying on **select international flights of Air India and IndiGo**.
- **Where it works:** terminal entry, the security hold area and boarding gates, wherever the airport has the equipment in place.
- **How to sign up:** in the Digi Yatra app, the passenger creates an e-passport-based credential. This means scanning the passport's machine-readable zone, reading the passport chip with the phone's NFC, taking a selfie and confirming the details. The credential is stored in the Digi Yatra wallet on the phone.
- **What does not change:** the ministry says the facility adds to existing processes and does not replace them. Passengers must still carry their travel documents and complete airline document checks, **immigration** and all other formalities.
- **What comes next:** feedback from the trial will shape a **wider rollout in 2027**. The ministry says Digi Yatra has more than 26.5 million users and has handled more than 110 million journeys across 39 domestic airports, with 25 more domestic airports planned by 2027.
- **IndiGo:** in its own [release of 1 November](https://www.goindigo.in/press-releases.html), IndiGo says a beta phase started at the end of October and runs through December 2026 on select IndiGo international flights from the same seven airports.

## Why it matters for India

This is the first time Indian outbound travellers can use Digi Yatra beyond domestic flights. During the trial it can only speed up entry, security and boarding. Immigration and airline document checks stay the same, so travellers should not plan on a shorter airport time yet. Only e-passport holders can join, and they need a phone that can read the passport chip. Agents with clients flying Air India or IndiGo from these seven airports can tell them about the option, but should add that it depends on which flights and airport touchpoints are included.
